## Part I: Understanding Spatial Computing

### 1.1 Definition and Core Concepts

**What is Spatial Computing?**

Spatial computing refers to the digitization of activities and interactions between machines, people, objects, and environments. It enables computing to happen in three-dimensional space, using the physical world as the interface.

**Core Technologies:**

**Mixed Reality (MR):**
- Blends physical and digital worlds
- Digital objects interact with physical environment
- Real-time environmental understanding
- Natural interaction paradigms

**Augmented Reality (AR):**
- Digital overlays on physical world
- Enhances rather than replaces reality
- Context-aware information display
- Mobile and glasses-based delivery

**Virtual Reality (VR):**
- Fully immersive digital environments
- Isolation from physical world
- Presence and embodiment
- High-fidelity sensory experiences

**Key Components:**

1. **Spatial Sensors:**
   - LiDAR for depth mapping
   - Computer vision cameras
   - IMUs for motion tracking
   - Hand/eye tracking sensors

2. **Compute Platform:**
   - Real-time 3D rendering
   - Spatial audio processing
   - AI/ML for scene understanding
   - Low-latency processing

3. **Display Technology:**
   - Micro-OLED/LED displays
   - Optical waveguides
   - Passthrough video
   - High refresh rates

4. **Interaction Paradigms:**
   - Gaze tracking
   - Hand gestures
   - Voice commands
   - Controller input
   - Brain signals (future)

## Appendices

### Appendix A: Technology Glossary

**6DOF:** Six degrees of freedom - full tracking of position and rotation
**Passthrough:** Video feed of real world shown in headset
**Foveated Rendering:** Rendering high detail only where user is looking
**Spatial Anchors:** Persistent 3D locations in physical space
**Hand Tracking:** Computer vision-based tracking of hand gestures
**Eye Tracking:** Detecting gaze direction
**Varifocal:** Dynamic focus adjustment matching eye accommodation
**Waveguide:** Optical technology for AR glasses
**LiDAR:** Light detection and ranging for depth sensing
**SLAM:** Simultaneous localization and mapping
